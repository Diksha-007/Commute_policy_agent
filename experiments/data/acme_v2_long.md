# Acme Corp Employee Transport Policy
<!-- company_id: acme | version: v2 | effective_date: 2026-10-01 -->

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose
This policy sets out how Acme Corp provides home-to-office transport for its employees. It aims to give every eligible employee a safe, punctual and fair commute. The transport desk at each Acme office administers this policy on behalf of the Administration department. This version replaces the policy dated 1 January 2026.

### 1.2 Applicability
This policy applies to all permanent employees of Acme Corp working from an Acme office in India. Contract staff and interns are covered only where their engagement letter says so. Where this policy conflicts with a local law, the local law prevails.

## 2. Eligibility

### 2.1 Eligibility criteria
Employees in grades L1 to L5 are eligible for company transport. The employee's registered home address must be more than 3 km from the office by road. Employees in grade L6 and above are not eligible and receive a conveyance allowance through payroll instead.

### 2.2 Change of address
An employee who moves home must update their address on the transport portal at least 3 working days before the change takes effect. Eligibility is re-checked against the new address. Routes are re-planned every Monday.

### 2.3 Eligibility verification and activation
The transport desk verifies grade, employment status, office assignment and registered residential address before activating an employee's transport profile. An employee must not use another person's profile or request a booking against an unverified address. Where records disagree, the employee must seek correction through the relevant HR process. Transport may remain inactive until the authoritative employee record is updated.

### 2.4 Eligibility changes and revalidation
A change in grade, employment status, office assignment or registered address may affect transport eligibility. The transport desk revalidates the employee's entitlement using the latest authoritative HR records and the road distance to the assigned office. A temporary assignment does not permanently change the employee's grade-based entitlement. Employees must report material changes promptly and must not continue using an allocation that they know is based on outdated information.

## 3. Night Shift

### 3.1 Shift slot timetable
Transport is planned only for the shift slots below. The drop time is when the cab leaves the office.

| Shift | Login | Logout | Drop departs | Escort for women employees |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early | 6:00 AM | 3:00 PM | 3:15 PM | No |
| General | 9:00 AM | 6:00 PM | 6:15 PM | No |
| Late general | 11:00 AM | 8:00 PM | 8:15 PM | Yes |
| Afternoon | 2:00 PM | 11:00 PM | 11:15 PM | Yes |
| Evening | 3:30 PM | 12:00 AM | 12:30 AM | Yes |
| Night | 10:00 PM | 7:00 AM | 7:15 AM | Yes, for the 9:00 PM pickup |

### 3.2 Security escort for women employees
A trained security guard travels in the cab on every drop that leaves the office after 8:00 PM when a woman employee is on board. The same applies to pickups before 6:00 AM. The guard sits in the front seat and stays in the cab until the last woman employee has been dropped.

The transport desk verifies the guard's identity card at the start of every trip. If no guard is available, the cab does not leave and the desk arranges a replacement. A woman employee is never the first pickup or the last drop on a route unless a guard is on board.

### 3.3 Last drop slot
The last scheduled drop leaves the office at 12:30 AM. Employees who work beyond the last drop slot need an ad-hoc trip under section 6.

### 3.4 Escort deployment and trip verification
The transport desk must record the escort assignment against each trip for which an escort is required. Before departure, the driver and escort must confirm the roster, route and employee count with the desk. Any discrepancy must be resolved before the vehicle leaves the office. A replacement escort must satisfy the same training and identity-verification requirements as the originally assigned escort.

### 3.5 Shift changes and roster updates
A shift change must be reflected in the employee roster before transport is reallocated. Employees must not board a vehicle assigned to a different shift solely because it is travelling to the same locality. The manager or authorised roster coordinator must communicate approved changes to the transport desk. Where a late operational change cannot be reflected in the roster, the desk must arrange a separately authorised conveyance rather than silently altering an existing route.

## 4. Booking

### 4.1 Booking cut-off
Employees must book or change a scheduled pickup or drop at least 2 hours before the trip on the transport portal. Requests made after the cut-off are treated as ad-hoc trips.

### 4.2 Cancellations
A trip can be cancelled without penalty up to 1 hour before the scheduled time. A trip cancelled later than that, or not cancelled at all, counts as a no-show under section 7.

### 4.3 Recurring bookings
Employees may request recurring transport for a period of up to 30 calendar days at a time. A recurring request must identify the applicable shift, pickup address, office location and working days. The transport desk validates the request against the employee's current eligibility and route capacity. A recurring booking is not a guarantee that every future trip will retain an identical pickup time; route adjustments must be communicated through the portal.

### 4.4 Booking confirmation and amendments
A booking is confirmed only when the transport portal displays an accepted status or the transport desk issues an equivalent recorded confirmation. A submitted request, draft or pending approval must not be treated as an allocated seat. Employees must review the confirmed pickup point and reporting time before each trip. Any change to the address, shift or required travel date must be submitted as an amendment and is subject to capacity and approval checks.

## 5. Weekends and Holidays

### 5.1 Weekend pickups
Saturday pickups and drops are available without any approval. Employees book them on the transport portal by Friday 6:00 PM. Transport is not provided on Sundays.

### 5.2 Public holidays
No transport is provided on public holidays. If a business unit declares a holiday as a working day, transport runs as on a Saturday.

### 5.3 Saturday booking validation
The transport desk validates Saturday bookings submitted through the portal by Friday 6:00 PM. The employee does not need reporting-manager approval for an ordinary Saturday booking. The desk checks the employee's eligibility, service date, shift, route and available vehicle capacity before confirming the allocation. A booking submitted after the stated cut-off is not guaranteed to be included in the Saturday roster.

### 5.4 Holiday roster confirmation
When a business unit declares a public holiday as a working day, its authorised roster coordinator must provide the transport desk with the affected employees, shift timings and office attendance requirement. The desk uses that roster to determine whether Saturday-style operations can be arranged. An office-opening notice alone does not confirm transport availability; the transport roster must separately confirm the booking.

## 6. Ad-hoc and Airport Trips

### 6.1 Approval for ad-hoc and airport trips
Ad-hoc trips, including trips to or from the airport for official travel, need two approvals before the trip: first the employee's reporting manager, then the transport desk. Both approvals are recorded on the transport portal. The trip is subject to the caps in section 8.

### 6.2 Ad-hoc request information
An ad-hoc request must state the business purpose, travel date, pickup and destination addresses, requested time, employee details and cost centre. Airport requests must additionally identify the official travel reference and the relevant flight schedule. The transport desk may return an incomplete request for correction. Reporting-manager approval alone does not authorise dispatch; the transport desk must also approve the operational allocation.

### 6.3 Urgent conveyance and retrospective records
Where an immediate operational requirement makes advance portal submission impracticable, the reporting manager and transport desk must provide recorded approval before the vehicle is dispatched. The employee must ensure that the request is regularised on the portal by the next working day, with the reason for urgency and the actual trip details. Retrospective documentation does not automatically validate an unapproved expense. Operational urgency does not remove the requirement for both approvals.

### 6.4 Approval sequence and dispatch control
The portal must record the reporting manager's decision before the request is presented for transport desk approval. The transport desk then checks the requested journey, vendor availability, applicable cost cap and safety requirements. A request that has only one approval remains pending and must not be treated as authorised for dispatch. The desk records its final decision and communicates the confirmed trip reference to the employee.

## 7. No-shows

### 7.1 No-show policy
A no-show is recorded when an employee is not at the pickup point within 5 minutes of the cab's arrival, or misses a drop without cancelling. The first no-show in a calendar month leads to a warning. A second no-show in the same month carries a fine of ₹200, recovered through payroll.

### 7.2 No-show penalty schedule
No-shows are counted per calendar month.

| No-shows in a month | Action | Notified |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warning | Employee |
| 2 | ₹200 fine | Employee, reporting manager |
| 3 | ₹500 fine and transport suspended for 1 week | Employee, reporting manager |
| 4 or more | Transport suspended for 1 month | Employee, reporting manager, HR |

### 7.3 Recording and disputing a no-show
The vendor must record the vehicle's arrival time and the employee's attendance outcome for each scheduled pickup. The transport desk reviews the record before applying a no-show consequence. An employee who disputes an entry must submit the trip date, route and reason through the transport portal within 5 working days of notification. The desk checks available roster and trip records and communicates its decision to the employee and reporting manager.

### 7.4 Payroll recovery and suspension administration
A no-show fine must be supported by the relevant trip record and the applicable monthly penalty schedule. The desk forwards an authorised recovery record to payroll with the employee and trip references. Where a transport suspension applies, the desk records its start and end dates and informs the employee and reporting manager. A fine or suspension must not be imposed solely on an unverified vendor assertion where the employee has raised a timely dispute.

## 8. Costs

### 8.1 Cost cap
The company pays up to ₹2,000 for a single ad-hoc trip. Any amount above the cap is recovered from the employee through payroll.

### 8.2 Cost caps by trip type

| Trip type | Cap per trip | Approver |
|---|---|---|
| Ad-hoc | ₹2,000 | Reporting manager and transport desk |
| Airport | ₹3,000 | Reporting manager and transport desk |
| Saturday | ₹1,500 | No approval needed |
| Guest | ₹3,500 | Vice President |
| Late stay beyond last drop | ₹1,000 | Transport desk |

### 8.3 Expense evidence and reconciliation
A vendor invoice must identify the trip reference, service date, route, vehicle allocation and amount charged. The transport desk checks the invoice against the approved booking and trip-completion record before forwarding it for payment. An invoice without sufficient supporting details may be held for clarification. Employees must not divide one journey into multiple requests to avoid a cap or approval threshold.

### 8.4 Recovery of excess amounts
Where an approved trip exceeds its applicable cap, the excess amount is handled according to the cost responsibility stated in this policy and the approved booking. For an ad-hoc trip governed by section 8.1, the excess is recovered from the employee through payroll. The employee must be given the trip reference and calculation supporting the recovery. A disputed recovery may be raised under the billing-dispute procedure in Chapter 18.

## 9. Guest Travel

### 9.1 Guest travel
Transport for visiting guests, such as candidates or client visitors, is allowed with approval from a Vice President. The host employee books the trip and is responsible for the guest during the trip.

### 9.2 Guest booking and identification
The host employee must provide the guest's name, visit purpose, destination, travel date and expected pickup time when requesting transport. The booking must identify the host employee and include the Vice President's approval. The transport desk may request confirmation of the guest's visit from the relevant office contact before allocating a vehicle. A guest must travel only on the trip recorded for that visit and must follow the driver's and transport desk's boarding instructions.

### 9.3 Guest cost accountability
Guest transport is subject to the per-trip cap and approval authority stated in section 8.2. The host employee's department bears responsibility for ensuring that the booking is correctly attributed and supported by the visit record. A host must not split a guest journey into separate bookings to bypass the cap. Any proposed expenditure above the applicable cap must be escalated for approval before dispatch and must not be presumed reimbursable merely because the guest is visiting for business purposes.

## 11. Hybrid and Remote Work Transport

### 11.1 Hybrid-day eligibility
An employee working under an approved hybrid arrangement may use scheduled transport only on a day recorded as an office-attendance day. The employee's attendance plan must be available to the transport desk before the route is finalised. A home-working day does not create an entitlement to a pickup or drop, even if a recurring booking exists. The employee must ensure that the portal booking matches the actual attendance plan.

### 11.2 Cancellation of hybrid-day transport
A hybrid-day booking must be cancelled at least 12 hours before the scheduled pickup when the employee changes from office attendance to remote work. A cancellation received after that cut-off may be recorded as a late cancellation and assessed under the applicable no-show and cost rules. Where the change is directed by the company after the cut-off, the employee must attach the manager's instruction so that the desk can distinguish an operational cancellation from an employee-initiated one.

### 11.3 Changes to attendance plans
Employees must update their attendance plan as soon as an approved change is known. The transport desk uses the latest authorised attendance record when preparing the daily roster. A calendar entry, informal team message or personal reminder does not replace the required attendance record. Where the portal and the approved attendance system show different information, the employee must seek correction from the responsible manager or HR contact before relying on a booking.

### 11.4 Remote-work exceptions
A remote employee may request transport for an exceptional office visit only where the visit is required for an authorised business purpose. The request must identify the approving manager, the office requirement and the requested journey. The transport desk treats the request as ad-hoc unless the employee's approved attendance plan has been updated in time for scheduled allocation. Any approved exception remains subject to the applicable cost cap and vehicle availability.

### 11.5 Hybrid roster publication
The transport desk prepares hybrid-day allocations from the attendance information available at the roster cut-off. Employees must check the published allocation for the correct service date, pickup point and shift. Where a recurring booking conflicts with a newly approved remote-work day, the employee must cancel or amend the affected trip rather than assume that the desk will infer the change. The desk records material roster amendments so that allocation decisions can be reviewed later.

### 11.6 Manager responsibilities for hybrid attendance
Reporting managers must ensure that office-attendance requirements are communicated through the approved attendance process. They should avoid confirming transport informally before the employee's attendance record is updated. Where a team meeting or operational requirement causes several employees to attend on an otherwise remote day, the manager should provide the desk with a consolidated request sufficiently early for route planning. Approval of office attendance does not by itself guarantee a seat.

### 11.7 Repeated hybrid booking discrepancies
The transport desk may refer repeated discrepancies between an employee's attendance plan and actual transport use to the reporting manager. The review should distinguish genuine operational changes from avoidable failure to amend bookings. Any adjustment to future allocations must be proportionate and communicated to the employee. The desk must not use a hybrid attendance discrepancy to alter grade eligibility or other entitlements that are governed by a separate provision of this policy.

## 12. Routes, Zones and Boarding Points

### 12.1 Route planning principles
Routes are planned using employee addresses, office destinations, shift timings, road conditions and available vehicle capacity. The transport desk seeks to provide a practical shared route while keeping the one-way journey within 90 minutes under normal operating conditions. A route may be adjusted when passenger demand, road access or a verified safety concern changes. Employees must use the route and boarding point shown in the confirmed roster.

### 12.2 Boarding-point distance
The ordinary walking distance between an employee's registered pickup location and the assigned boarding point must not exceed 500 metres. The desk assesses the practical pedestrian route rather than relying only on straight-line distance. Where a road closure, access restriction or safety concern makes the normal point unsuitable, the desk may assign an alternative point and record the reason. An employee must not independently change the boarding point with the driver.

### 12.3 Zone allocation
The transport desk may divide an office's service area into route zones to coordinate shared journeys. Zone boundaries are administrative planning tools and do not independently determine grade eligibility or the employee's registered home address. An employee whose address falls near a boundary must provide the complete address and any relevant access information for verification. The desk may revise zone assignments when demand or road conditions materially change.

### 12.4 Route publication and employee notification
The desk publishes confirmed route information through the transport portal or another approved company channel. The notice should identify the service date, boarding point, expected reporting time and route reference. Employees are responsible for reviewing the latest notice before travelling. Where a route changes after publication, the desk must communicate the revised information to affected employees and retain a record of the change.

### 12.5 Journey-duration monitoring
The transport desk monitors scheduled and actual journey durations to identify routes that repeatedly approach or exceed the 90-minute one-way limit. A single delay caused by an unusual road incident must be distinguished from a recurring planning problem. Where the limit is repeatedly exceeded under ordinary conditions, the desk must review the route sequence, passenger allocation and departure time and document the corrective action.

### 12.6 Safe boarding-point selection
A boarding point should allow passengers to wait without obstructing traffic and should provide a practical place for a vehicle to stop. The desk may reject a requested location where road geometry, restricted access, construction or a documented safety concern makes boarding unsuitable. Employees must wait at the assigned point and must not signal a vehicle to stop at an unapproved location. Any concern about the suitability of a point should be reported before the next roster is finalised.

### 12.7 Route-change requests
An employee may request a route or boarding-point review by submitting the reason and relevant address details through the portal. The desk evaluates the request against the 500-metre walking limit, journey duration, passenger sequence and safety conditions. A request is not effective until the revised allocation is confirmed. Employees must continue using the existing confirmed point unless the desk communicates a replacement or an immediate safety instruction requires otherwise.

## 13. Vehicles and Drivers

### 13.1 Vehicle eligibility
Vehicles assigned to employee transport must satisfy applicable registration, fitness, insurance and permit requirements. A vehicle must not be allocated if required documents have expired or cannot be verified. The maximum permitted vehicle age for this service is five years, calculated from the date of first registration. The vendor must provide current vehicle details to the transport desk before the vehicle is added to the approved fleet.

### 13.2 Driver eligibility and verification
A driver must hold a valid licence appropriate to the assigned vehicle and must complete the vendor's identity and employment verification process. The transport desk or vendor coordinator must verify the driver assigned to a trip against the roster before dispatch. A substitute driver must undergo the same checks and must be recorded against the trip. An employee must report a mismatch between the assigned driver and the person presenting for pickup.

### 13.3 Driver duty limits
A driver must not be assigned more than 10 hours of driving duty in a day under the Acme employee transport service. The vendor is responsible for scheduling adequate rest and arranging a replacement when the duty limit would otherwise be exceeded. The transport desk may reject a proposed allocation where the duty record is incomplete or indicates that the driver has reached the limit. Operational urgency does not authorise a vendor to disregard this requirement.

### 13.4 Vehicle condition inspections
The vendor must maintain a documented inspection process covering tyres, brakes, lights, seat belts, doors, mirrors and other safety-critical equipment. A vehicle with a defect that may affect safe operation must be withdrawn until the defect is rectified and the vehicle is cleared for service. The transport desk may request inspection evidence or arrange a spot check. Employees must report visible defects before departure where it is safe and practical to do so.

### 13.5 Passenger capacity and seating
The number of passengers assigned to a vehicle must not exceed its legally permitted seating capacity. Every passenger must use an available designated seat and seat belt. The driver must not accept additional passengers who are absent from the approved roster unless the transport desk has authorised the change. A request to carry an unlisted person must be referred to the desk, which will verify eligibility or guest approval before changing the allocation.

### 13.6 Driver conduct
Drivers must follow the confirmed route, observe traffic rules and communicate with the transport desk when a material delay or disruption occurs. They must not use a handheld device while driving, solicit personal payments from employees or disclose passenger information to unauthorised persons. Employees must raise conduct concerns through the designated reporting channel rather than attempting to resolve a serious safety issue while the vehicle is moving.

### 13.7 Vehicle substitution and breakdowns
If an assigned vehicle becomes unavailable or develops a defect, the vendor must notify the transport desk and arrange a compliant replacement. The replacement must satisfy the same age, documentation, capacity and safety requirements as the original allocation. The desk must record the substitute vehicle and communicate material changes to affected passengers. A breakdown must not be used as a reason to transfer employees into an unverified or over-capacity vehicle.

## 14. Safety, Incidents and Emergencies

### 14.1 General duty of care
Employees, drivers, escorts and vendors must act in a manner that protects passenger safety throughout the journey. Employees must board and alight only at the assigned point unless the transport desk issues an alternative instruction. Drivers must not begin a trip where a required safety control is missing. Any person who identifies an immediate danger should alert the driver and transport desk as soon as it is safe to do so.

### 14.2 Incident reporting deadline
An employee or vendor who becomes aware of an accident, injury, security concern or other material transport incident must notify the transport desk within 2 hours of becoming aware of it. The initial report should state the trip reference, time, location, people involved and immediate action taken. The reporting deadline does not require a person to delay emergency assistance in order to complete a form. Further details may be submitted after urgent needs are addressed.

### 14.3 Emergency response and medical expenditure
In a medical emergency during an authorised company transport trip, the driver or escort must prioritise immediate assistance and contact appropriate emergency services where necessary. The transport desk must be informed as soon as practicable. Eligible emergency medical expenditure may be supported up to ₹2,500 per incident, subject to the applicable evidence and review process. This cap is not a substitute for emergency care or a reason to delay treatment.

### 14.4 Accident scene procedure
Following a road accident, the driver must stop at a safe location, secure the vehicle where possible and seek emergency assistance for anyone injured. The driver must not leave the scene except where required to obtain urgent assistance or directed by competent authorities. The vendor must promptly notify the transport desk and preserve trip records, vehicle details and relevant incident evidence. Employees should provide factual information and avoid speculation about responsibility while the incident is being assessed.

### 14.5 Security concerns during a trip
An employee who experiences threatening conduct, suspected unauthorised access or an unsafe route deviation should contact the transport desk using the approved reporting channel. If there is an immediate threat to life or safety, the employee should seek assistance from emergency services first. The desk must record the concern, identify the trip and vendor personnel involved, and determine whether a replacement vehicle, escort or other protective measure is required before the journey continues.

### 14.6 Emergency contacts and escalation
The transport desk must maintain an accessible escalation procedure for incidents occurring during service hours and outside ordinary office hours. The procedure must identify the responsible desk role, the vendor control contact and the company role authorised to coordinate urgent assistance. Contact details must be reviewed periodically and communicated to drivers and escorts. An employee should use the emergency channel for an immediate safety concern and the ordinary grievance channel for non-urgent service dissatisfaction.

### 14.7 Incident review and corrective action
The Administration department reviews material incidents to determine whether the response was timely and whether any process, vehicle, route or vendor control requires correction. The review should distinguish verified facts from preliminary accounts and should record actions, owners and target dates. Where an incident reveals a recurring risk, the desk may temporarily suspend the affected allocation pending review. Closure of an incident record must not prevent a later review when new evidence becomes available.

## 15. Medical and Special Needs Transport

### 15.1 Temporary medical accommodation
An employee who requires a temporary transport accommodation for a medical reason may submit a request through the transport portal. The request should identify the practical assistance required, the expected duration and any relevant travel restriction. Medical details must be limited to information necessary to assess the accommodation. The transport desk coordinates with the appropriate HR or Administration role and communicates the approved arrangement to the vendor without disclosing unnecessary personal information.

### 15.2 Pregnancy-related door-to-door service
From the 24th week of pregnancy, an eligible employee may request door-to-door pickup and drop instead of using the ordinary boarding point. The request must be submitted to the transport desk with confirmation through the approved HR process. The desk records the effective date and informs the vendor of the revised pickup and drop arrangement. The service remains subject to vehicle availability and safe road access, and any alternative arrangement must be discussed with the employee.

### 15.3 Mobility and accessibility requirements
An employee with a mobility limitation may request an accessible boarding location or other reasonable transport adjustment. The desk assesses the building entrance, vehicle access, route conditions and available fleet capability before confirming the arrangement. The employee should describe the functional assistance required rather than provide unrelated medical history. Where the existing vendor cannot meet the requirement safely, the desk escalates the matter to Administration for an alternative arrangement.

### 15.4 Medical appointment transport
Transport for a routine medical appointment is not automatically included in scheduled home-to-office service. An employee may request an official trip where the appointment is connected to a company-directed occupational health requirement or another approved business process. The request must identify the approving authority and destination. Personal medical appointments should be arranged independently unless a specific accommodation has been approved under the company's applicable HR process.

### 15.5 Emergency assistance for an employee
If an employee becomes unwell during a journey, the driver must stop at a safe location and seek appropriate assistance. The transport desk should be informed as soon as practicable and should coordinate with the employee's emergency contact or company representative where authorised. Immediate medical attention takes precedence over ordinary route completion. Any eligible emergency expenditure is subject to the ₹2,500 per-incident cap and the evidence requirements described in Chapter 14 and Chapter 18.

### 15.6 Confidential handling of accommodation requests
Medical and accessibility information must be shared only with personnel who need it to arrange or administer the approved service. The transport desk should communicate operational instructions to the vendor without forwarding unnecessary medical documents. Records must be stored in an approved company system with access controls. A manager or vendor must not request additional medical information directly from an employee where the transport desk can assess the practical requirement without it.

### 15.7 Review of continuing arrangements
A temporary accommodation should be reviewed when its approved period ends or when the employee reports a material change in requirement. The desk may contact the employee through the approved company channel to confirm whether the arrangement remains necessary. A review must not automatically cancel an accommodation before the employee has been notified and an appropriate alternative has been considered. Any extension should be recorded with a revised effective period.

## 16. Official and Special Trips

### 16.1 Official inter-office journeys
An employee travelling between Acme offices for an authorised business purpose may request transport through the official trip process. The request must state the originating office, destination office, business purpose, date and required arrival time. The reporting manager must approve the journey before dispatch. Where the journey is part of a larger official itinerary, the employee should include the relevant travel reference so that the desk can reconcile the service and its cost.

### 16.2 Airport transfers for official travel
Airport transfers are permitted only when connected to approved official travel and supported by the two approvals required under section 6.1. The employee must provide the flight details, terminal where known, pickup address and requested reporting time. The transport desk should plan a reasonable buffer for traffic and airport access restrictions. The applicable airport cap is ₹3,000 per trip unless a higher amount has been separately authorised under the exception process.

### 16.3 Special business events
Transport for an approved company event, training programme or business meeting may be arranged where the organiser submits a consolidated requirement. The request should include the event location, schedule, attendee count, pickup areas and cost centre. The event organiser must identify the approving manager and nominate a coordinator for changes on the service date. An event booking does not override passenger capacity, driver duty limits or vehicle safety requirements.

### 16.4 Multi-stop official itineraries
A journey requiring multiple official stops must be submitted as one itinerary wherever the portal permits. Each stop should have a stated business purpose and an expected arrival or departure time. The transport desk evaluates the proposed sequence for route feasibility and cost treatment. Employees must not ask a driver to add an unapproved stop during the trip. A material itinerary change requires approval and an updated trip record before the additional journey is undertaken.

### 16.5 Special access and restricted premises
Where an official destination is within a secured, restricted or controlled-access premises, the employee or event organiser must provide the entry requirements before the vehicle is dispatched. The transport desk communicates any verified access instruction to the vendor. Drivers must comply with lawful site procedures and must not attempt unauthorised entry. If access is refused, the driver should contact the desk for a revised instruction rather than leave the vehicle or passengers in an unsafe location.

### 16.6 Cancellation of official trips
An official trip that is no longer required must be cancelled through the portal as soon as the organiser becomes aware of the change. The ordinary cancellation cut-off remains applicable unless a company-directed change or documented operational emergency justifies a different treatment. The desk records the cancellation time and any vendor charge supported by the contract. Approval of the original trip does not automatically approve a cancellation fee or a replacement journey.

### 16.7 Reconciliation of special-trip records
The organiser must confirm whether the approved trip was completed, partially completed or cancelled. The transport desk reconciles the trip reference with the vendor's service record and invoice. Any variance in route, duration, passenger count or amount must be referred for clarification before payment. Records for special trips must identify the approving authority and cost centre so that the expenditure can be reviewed independently of ordinary scheduled transport.

## 17. Vendors and Service Levels

### 17.1 Vendor appointment
Vendors may provide employee transport only after completing the company's onboarding and due-diligence process. The review includes legal registration, permits, insurance, fleet records, driver verification, safety procedures and financial documentation. The transport desk must maintain an approved-vendor register and must not allocate a trip to an unapproved provider. Appointment of a vendor does not remove Acme's right to inspect service records or require corrective action.

### 17.2 On-time pickup performance
The vendor's monthly on-time pickup target is 95%. The transport desk measures performance against the confirmed pickup time, applying only documented exclusions approved under the service agreement. A pickup is not considered on time merely because the vehicle reaches the general locality; it must reach the assigned boarding point within the agreed tolerance. Repeated failure to meet the target requires a documented service review and corrective plan.

### 17.3 Vehicle and driver availability
The vendor must maintain sufficient compliant vehicles and verified drivers to support the confirmed roster. It must inform the transport desk promptly when a vehicle or driver becomes unavailable and provide a replacement that meets this policy. A substitute must not be deployed before its identity and compliance details are recorded. The vendor is responsible for ensuring that replacement arrangements do not cause a breach of vehicle-age, capacity or driver-duty requirements.

### 17.4 Vendor incident notification
A vendor must immediately notify the transport desk of a serious accident, passenger injury, security incident, vehicle breakdown or material service interruption. The vendor must provide an initial factual account and preserve relevant trip, driver and vehicle records. The employee's two-hour incident-reporting deadline does not reduce the vendor's obligation to notify the desk promptly. The vendor must cooperate with the company's review and provide supporting documentation when requested.

### 17.5 Performance review and service correction
The transport desk reviews vendor performance using punctuality, complaint trends, trip completion, safety compliance, invoice accuracy and record completeness. A review may be conducted monthly or more frequently where service quality deteriorates. The vendor must provide an action plan for material deficiencies, with a responsible owner and completion date for each action. The desk records whether corrective measures were effective and may escalate unresolved failures under the vendor agreement.

### 17.6 Vendor personnel conduct
Vendor personnel must treat employees and guests respectfully and must follow the company's safety, privacy and access requirements. A driver or coordinator must not solicit cash, gifts or personal favours from passengers. The vendor must investigate substantiated conduct complaints and provide the desk with the outcome and corrective action. Where an allegation indicates an immediate safety or integrity risk, the desk may require the individual to be removed from Acme service pending review.

### 17.7 Audit and records access
Vendors must retain the fleet, driver, trip, attendance, incident and invoice records required by the service agreement. The transport desk may inspect relevant records to verify compliance with this policy and the agreed service levels. Records must be made available within the period specified in the contract or a reasonable period set by the desk. A vendor's failure to maintain or produce required records may be treated as a service deficiency and escalated to Administration.

## 18. Billing, Charges and Reimbursement

### 18.1 Invoice validation
The transport desk validates vendor invoices against approved bookings, trip-completion records, applicable caps and contractual rates. Each invoice line must identify a traceable trip reference and service date. Charges that cannot be reconciled must be placed on hold while the vendor supplies clarification. The desk must not approve duplicate charges or amounts unsupported by the booking and service record.

### 18.2 Employee charge notification
Where an employee is responsible for an excess amount or another authorised charge, the employee must receive a written explanation before payroll recovery is initiated. The notice should identify the trip, applicable policy provision, total approved cost, relevant cap and calculation of the amount proposed for recovery. The employee may raise a billing dispute through the portal. A charge must not be increased by an undocumented administrative fee.

### 18.3 Billing dispute window
An employee or department disputing a transport charge must raise the dispute within 30 calendar days of receiving the relevant charge statement or invoice notification. The dispute should include the trip reference, amount questioned and supporting explanation. The transport desk reviews the booking, approval, vendor invoice and available trip records before communicating an outcome. A dispute submitted within the prescribed window must be recorded and tracked until a decision is issued.

### 18.4 Departmental cost allocation
Where this policy assigns a trip cost to an employee's department, the transport desk must use the cost centre recorded in the approved request. The department is responsible for checking that the trip was authorised and correctly attributed. A change in cost centre after service completion requires a documented correction and confirmation from the relevant finance authority. The employee must not be charged personally where the policy assigns the amount to the department.

### 18.5 Medical emergency expenditure evidence
A request for support of eligible medical expenditure arising from an emergency during an authorised transport trip must include the trip reference, incident date, nature of the immediate assistance and available receipts. Reimbursement or direct settlement is limited to ₹2,500 per incident under this policy. The desk may request clarification where the documents do not establish the connection to the transport incident. The employee's access to urgent medical care must never be delayed while evidence is being collected.

### 18.6 Reimbursement review and payment status
The responsible finance or Administration team reviews a complete reimbursement submission under the company's payment process. The transport desk may confirm trip details but does not independently authorise payment outside its delegated authority. The employee should receive a status update when the submission is accepted, returned for clarification or declined. Any rejection must identify the policy basis and the channel through which the employee may seek a review.

### 18.7 Financial record retention
The transport desk and vendor must retain invoices, approvals, trip records, dispute outcomes and recovery calculations in the approved record system. Access must be limited to personnel who require the information for service administration, finance or audit. Records must be sufficiently detailed to reconstruct the basis of a charge without retaining unrelated personal information. Disposal must follow the applicable company retention schedule and any legal hold.

## 19. Data, Privacy and Tracking

### 19.1 Purpose of transport data collection
Acme collects employee transport information to determine eligibility, plan routes, allocate vehicles, administer bookings, support safety and reconcile service costs. Information may include an employee's name, grade, registered address, shift, pickup point, trip history and booking status. The transport desk must use this information only for authorised transport administration and related company purposes. Data must not be collected merely because a vendor requests it.

### 19.2 Location tracking during service
Vehicle location information may be used while a company transport trip is active to monitor service progress, investigate delays and support emergency response. Access must be limited to authorised transport personnel and other approved roles with a legitimate operational need. Tracking must not be repurposed for unrelated employee surveillance. The desk should ensure that vendor systems are configured consistently with the approved service arrangement.

### 19.3 Location-data retention
Trip-related location data must ordinarily be retained for 90 calendar days after the relevant journey. Where an incident, dispute, audit or legal requirement requires preservation, the relevant records may be retained for the period necessary to resolve that matter. The reason for an extended retention period should be recorded. At the end of the applicable period, location records must be securely deleted or de-identified under the approved process.

### 19.4 Access to personal information
Employees may request access to transport information associated with their bookings through the designated company channel. The desk verifies the requester's identity and provides information that may appropriately be disclosed. Information relating to another passenger, driver or employee must not be shared without a valid basis. Requests involving correction of the registered home address must be routed through the authoritative HR or employee-record process.

### 19.5 Vendor data handling
A vendor may process employee information only to the extent necessary to provide the contracted transport service. The vendor must protect access credentials, restrict personnel access and prevent unauthorised copying or disclosure. Information must not be used to contact employees for marketing or personal purposes. When a vendor's service ends, it must return or securely dispose of company information as required by the agreement and applicable retention obligations.

### 19.6 Data correction and suspected disclosure
An employee who identifies an inaccurate transport record or suspects unauthorised disclosure should report it through the designated company channel. The transport desk must refer privacy concerns to the appropriate company function and preserve relevant evidence. Corrections to identity, grade or registered address must be made in the authoritative source system before dependent transport records are amended. The desk should communicate the outcome to the employee where permitted.

### 19.7 Confidentiality of route information
Route sheets, passenger lists, residential addresses and trip histories are confidential operational records. Drivers and vendor coordinators may access only the information required to complete their assigned service. They must not photograph, retain or circulate passenger lists for personal purposes. The transport desk must avoid distributing complete route data to employees when a passenger-specific confirmation is sufficient.

## 20. Feedback, Complaints and Escalation

### 20.1 Submitting a complaint
An employee may submit a complaint about punctuality, route allocation, driver conduct, vehicle condition, escort arrangements, billing or another transport-service matter through the transport portal. The complaint should include the trip reference, date, location and a factual description of the concern. Supporting evidence may be attached where available. Immediate safety risks must be reported through the emergency channel rather than waiting for ordinary complaint processing.

### 20.2 Complaint acknowledgement
The transport desk must acknowledge receipt of a complaint within 24 hours. The acknowledgement should provide a reference number and identify the channel through which further information may be requested. Acknowledgement confirms receipt, not acceptance of the allegation or completion of the investigation. Where the matter is urgent, the desk must take appropriate interim action without waiting for the ordinary review timeline.

### 20.3 Complaint investigation
The desk reviews the complaint against available booking, roster, vendor, vehicle and trip records. It may seek a factual response from the vendor or relevant personnel and should give the complainant a reasonable opportunity to clarify material details. The investigation must be impartial and proportionate to the issue raised. Personal information unrelated to the complaint should not be circulated during the review.

### 20.4 Escalation of unresolved complaints
An employee who considers the transport desk's response incomplete may request escalation to the Administration department. The escalation should identify the original complaint reference, the response received and the specific issue that remains unresolved. Matters involving employee conduct, discrimination, privacy or other specialist concerns may be referred to the appropriate company function. The transport desk must record the referral and avoid duplicating an investigation already assigned to another authorised team.

### 20.5 Protection against retaliation
An employee must not be disadvantaged for raising a complaint in good faith or reporting a genuine safety concern. Managers and vendors must not discourage employees from using the established reporting channels. Knowingly fabricated complaints may be addressed through the applicable company process, but an allegation must not be treated as false merely because it cannot be substantiated. The desk should preserve confidentiality to the extent reasonably possible.

### 20.6 Complaint closure
A complaint may be closed after the desk has recorded the review undertaken, the findings, any corrective action and the communication sent to the complainant. Where a matter is referred to another company function, the desk should record the referral rather than represent the specialist review as completed. Reopened complaints must be linked to the original reference and should identify the new information or unresolved issue that justifies further review.

### 20.7 Trend analysis
The transport desk periodically reviews complaint categories, recurrence, affected routes and vendor patterns to identify systemic service weaknesses. Trend analysis should use only the personal information necessary to understand and correct the issue. Findings may inform route redesign, vendor review, driver training or changes to operational controls. A recurring complaint should not be treated as an isolated matter solely because each individual trip has a separate reference.

## 21. Weather and Service Disruptions

### 21.1 Weather suspension trigger
Transport operations may be suspended when the competent weather authority issues a red alert for an affected service area. The transport desk assesses the alert, local road conditions and available safety information before confirming the operational response. Where suspension is required, the desk informs affected employees and vendors through the approved communication channel. A vendor must not continue a route merely because a vehicle has already been allocated.

### 21.2 Localised road disruption
A road closure, flooding, landslide, major traffic restriction or other local disruption may require a route to be delayed, diverted or cancelled even when no area-wide weather suspension has been declared. The driver must stop or divert only where it is safe and lawful to do so and must notify the transport desk. The desk communicates the revised plan to affected passengers and records material route changes.

### 21.3 Employee notification during disruption
When service is materially delayed, diverted or suspended, the transport desk should notify affected employees with the known status, expected next update and any action required from them. Employees must monitor the approved communication channel and must not rely on an outdated pickup notification. If the desk cannot provide a reliable revised time, it should state that the allocation remains unconfirmed rather than provide an unsupported estimate.

### 21.4 Vehicle breakdown and replacement
A vehicle breakdown must be reported to the transport desk immediately. The vendor arranges a replacement vehicle that satisfies the policy's safety and compliance requirements and provides an estimated arrival time when it can reasonably do so. Passengers must remain in a safe location and follow instructions from the driver or desk. The replacement journey must be recorded against the original trip reference or linked to it for audit.

### 21.5 Office closure and emergency directions
If an office is closed or an attendance requirement is withdrawn because of an emergency, the relevant company authority must communicate the decision to the transport desk. The desk then determines which scheduled trips should be cancelled, curtailed or replaced. Employees must follow the official company instruction and should not independently instruct a driver to continue a route to a closed site. Any required return journey must be separately recorded.

### 21.6 Resumption of service
Transport may resume after the desk has reviewed the relevant alert, road conditions and operational guidance. The resumption notice should identify the affected service area, effective time and any route-specific restrictions. A general improvement in weather does not automatically reactivate every suspended route. The desk must obtain confirmation that the vendor can safely operate before publishing a revised roster.

### 21.7 Disruption records and cost treatment
The desk records the reason for a material disruption, the routes affected, the notifications issued and the service decision taken. Vendor charges arising from a cancelled or interrupted journey must be checked against the service agreement and the actual work performed. Employees must not be charged for a disruption-related cancellation without a valid policy basis and supporting record. Dispute rights under Chapter 18 remain available.

## 22. Festivals and Peak Days

### 22.1 Peak-day planning
The transport desk may prepare a special roster for festivals, major office events, high-attendance days or periods when ordinary route demand is expected to change. Business units should submit attendance estimates, shift requirements and office operating hours within the planning timeline communicated by the desk. The desk uses this information to assess vehicle capacity and vendor availability. A peak-day plan does not automatically override the normal eligibility requirements.

### 22.2 Advance passenger confirmation
Employees included in a special roster must confirm their attendance and transport requirement by the stated deadline. The desk may release unconfirmed allocations where demand exceeds available capacity, subject to the applicable eligibility and priority rules. Employees must check the final roster rather than rely on a preliminary event notice. Any late addition is subject to approval, capacity and the applicable booking procedure.

### 22.3 Altered office schedules
Where a business unit changes its working hours for a festival or peak day, the authorised roster coordinator must communicate the revised shift timings to the transport desk. The desk determines whether existing routes can be adjusted or whether separate allocations are required. Employees must not assume that ordinary shift departure times apply to a special schedule. The confirmed trip notice is the authoritative source for the service date.

### 22.4 Demand prioritisation
If available vehicles cannot accommodate all eligible requests on a peak day, the desk applies documented operational priorities approved by Administration. Prioritisation may consider shift necessity, safety requirements, approved accessibility arrangements and the feasibility of shared routes. The desk should record material allocation decisions and communicate any request that cannot be accommodated. No employee may obtain priority by making an unauthorised payment to a vendor or driver.

### 22.5 Temporary boarding arrangements
High attendance or temporary road restrictions may require the desk to designate a temporary boarding point for a peak day. The revised point must be assessed for pedestrian access and safe vehicle stopping and should ordinarily remain within the 500-metre walking limit. Employees must receive the location and reporting time before the trip. A temporary arrangement must not be treated as a permanent change to the employee's registered address or regular route.

### 22.6 Vendor capacity confirmation
Before publishing a peak-day roster, the desk obtains confirmation that the vendor can provide the required compliant vehicles, drivers and any necessary escorts. The vendor must not meet increased demand by exceeding passenger capacity or driver duty limits. If the confirmed capacity falls short, the desk informs the relevant business units and seeks an approved alternative. The final allocation must reflect actual confirmed capacity rather than an unverified estimate.

### 22.7 Post-event reconciliation
After a special service day, the desk reconciles the planned roster with completed, cancelled and amended trips. The review should identify unserved requests, material delays, vendor substitutions and additional costs. The organiser may be asked to confirm attendance where it affects cost allocation. Lessons from the event should be recorded before the next comparable peak period so that planning assumptions can be improved.

## 23. Exceptions and Policy Administration

### 23.1 Exception request
An employee or reporting manager seeking a departure from this policy must submit a written exception request before the affected trip or arrangement begins, except where an immediate emergency makes prior submission impracticable. The request must identify the provision, reason, duration, affected employees and proposed control measures. The transport desk records the request and forwards it to the authorised approver. An exception is effective only after approval is recorded.

### 23.2 Exception approval authority
The Head of Administration is the designated approver for policy exceptions. The reporting manager may recommend an exception but cannot approve a departure from a policy requirement unless a separate written delegation expressly permits it. Vendor personnel must not treat an employee's verbal assurance as an exception. The desk must retain the approval and communicate its scope, effective period and any conditions to the affected parties.

### 23.3 Emergency deviation
Where an immediate threat to life or safety requires a temporary deviation from an ordinary operational procedure, the driver or transport desk may take the minimum action necessary to protect passengers and obtain assistance. The deviation must be reported to the desk as soon as practicable and documented with the reason, time and action taken. This provision does not authorise routine disregard of licensing, vehicle safety, passenger capacity or other mandatory legal requirements.

### 23.4 Exception register
The transport desk maintains a register of approved and declined exceptions. Each entry should identify the requestor, policy provision, business reason, approver, effective period and conditions imposed. The register enables Administration to identify repeated requests and determine whether a policy clarification is needed. Expired exceptions must not be treated as continuing approvals, and a new request is required where the underlying circumstances materially change.

### 23.5 Policy interpretation
Questions about the meaning or application of this policy should first be directed to the transport desk. Matters involving eligibility records may be referred to HR, while matters involving cost treatment may be referred to the responsible finance authority. Where a question requires a formal policy interpretation, the desk escalates it to Administration. A vendor's informal interpretation does not amend or override the published policy.

### 23.6 Review and amendment
The Administration department reviews this policy periodically and when a material legal, operational or safety change requires an update. Amendments must be approved through the company's policy governance process and published with an effective date. The transport desk must communicate material changes to employees and vendors and update related operating procedures. A draft amendment or verbal announcement does not replace the currently effective published version.

### 23.7 Relationship with local law
All transport arrangements must comply with applicable central, state and local legal requirements. Where a legal requirement provides a higher standard of protection or conflicts with a provision of this policy, the legal requirement prevails to the extent of the conflict. The transport desk should refer uncertainty about a legal obligation to the appropriate company authority rather than ask a vendor or employee to decide the issue independently.

## 24. Roles and Contacts

### 24.1 Employee responsibilities
Employees must maintain accurate transport details, book within the prescribed timelines, use the confirmed boarding point and follow reasonable safety instructions. They must cancel trips they no longer require and report incidents or service concerns through the approved channels. Employees must not request unauthorised route changes, carry unlisted passengers or make personal payments to drivers. Each employee is responsible for checking the latest confirmed trip information before departure.

### 24.2 Reporting manager responsibilities
The reporting manager confirms business need where approval is required, supports accurate attendance and shift information, and reviews transport requests submitted by team members. The manager must approve ad-hoc and airport requests before they are submitted to the transport desk. Managers must not promise a transport allocation before the desk confirms capacity. They should also support timely resolution of employee concerns that require a business or cost-centre decision.

### 24.3 Transport desk responsibilities
The transport desk administers eligibility checks, route planning, roster publication, vendor coordination, booking changes, incident intake and service-level monitoring. It maintains the records needed to support allocation, billing and complaint review. The desk must communicate material service changes and escalate matters beyond its delegated authority. It is also responsible for ensuring that the operational instructions issued to vendors are consistent with this policy.

### 24.4 Administration department responsibilities
Administration oversees policy implementation, vendor governance, exception decisions and material service escalations. It reviews transport performance and determines whether operational controls or policy provisions require amendment. Administration coordinates with HR, Finance, Security and other relevant functions when an issue crosses departmental responsibilities. It must ensure that exceptions and significant corrective actions are properly authorised and recorded.

### 24.5 Vendor responsibilities
The vendor supplies compliant vehicles and verified drivers, maintains required service records and operates within the agreed contract and this policy. It must notify the transport desk of breakdowns, incidents, substitutions and material delays. The vendor is responsible for driver conduct, vehicle maintenance and compliance with duty limits. It must protect employee information and cooperate with audits, investigations and corrective-action reviews.

### 24.6 HR and Finance responsibilities
HR maintains authoritative employee information relevant to grade, employment status and approved work arrangements. It supports the transport desk where employee-record corrections or specialist employee matters arise. Finance or the designated financial authority reviews invoices, cost-centre allocations, recoveries and reimbursements under the company's financial controls. Neither function should rely solely on an informal vendor statement where a recorded booking or approval is required.

### 24.7 Contact and escalation channels
The transport desk must publish the current company-approved contact channel for ordinary bookings, urgent service issues and complaints. The escalation procedure must identify the responsible role for after-hours incidents and the route for referring privacy, employee-relations or financial matters to specialist teams. Contact information must be reviewed when responsibilities change. Employees should use the channel appropriate to the urgency and subject of the issue.
